I presented *The impact of depression and treatment uptake on labour productivity* at a workshop organised by MUCHE and the Lifespan Health and Wellbeing Research Centre at Macquarie University.

The presentation uses linked Australian administrative data spanning 2017–18 to 2022–23 and a stacked event-study design to trace earnings dynamics around first observed antidepressant dispensing. We find that annual earnings fall sharply at treatment initiation and remain below pre-initiation levels for several years, with larger and more persistent declines for men. The results highlight how administrative data can help us understand health trajectories and causal relationships when experimentation is not possible.

The workshop also featured Fabio Martinenghi's talk, *The Concentrated Cost of Coercion: Labour-Market Consequences of Vaccine Mandates for Non-Compliers*, on the labour-market effects of Western Australia's COVID-19 vaccination mandates. A big thank you to Fabio for visiting us and giving such an engaging talk—I was very happy to host him at Macquarie.
